The Samsung 990 PRO is rated for up to 7,450 MB/s reads and 6,900 MB/s writes, close to the ceiling of a PCIe 4.0 x4 link. It also has a dedicated DRAM cache and a five-year warranty. On paper, it’s still one of the most complete drives in its class.

But the better question isn’t “is it fast?” It’s “will your workload and your wallet notice?”

Where you’ll feel it. Large file copies, video editing and project transfers, and installing or moving big game libraries all benefit from strong write speeds and a DRAM cache.

Where you won’t. Booting Windows, opening apps and browsing depend more on your CPU, RAM and the operating system than on sequential speed. A good PCIe 3.0 drive can feel almost identical.

Don’t forget the practicalities:

Cooling. The bare drive is thin and made for laptops and desktops. In a PS5 you need a compatible heatsink and must check Sony’s size limits first.
Slot support. It works in a PCIe 3.0 slot, but it will run at that slot’s speed, so you’d be paying for performance you don’t use.
Endurance. The 1,200 TBW rating on the 2TB model works out to roughly 657 GB of writes per day over five years, far beyond what most home users write. Warranty ratings aren’t a prediction of lifespan, though.

And watch the price. SSD prices rose through 2026, and in some markets PCIe 5.0 drives sit close to the 990 PRO’s price. Check same-day quotes for the 990 PRO, the 990 EVO Plus, the WD Black SN850X and a PCIe 5.0 option at the same capacity before deciding.
